# Mode: topic

A topic the user names, at most 200 characters, stored as the run's `topic`. With no topic given, ask for one; never pick one.

## What to find

- **Definitions**: how the field defines the topic, from standards bodies, official documentation or the people who coined the term.
- **The current state**: what holds now, with dated sources.
- **Data points**: a figure only when it is in a quote. Prefer the study or the report itself to an article about it.
- **Opposing views**: where credible sources disagree, each side attributed to its source.
- **Dated events**: releases, announcements and changes, each with the date its source gives.

## Primary sources first

The study, the standard, the announcement and the documentation come first. A secondary source that reports a primary one is cited only when the primary one cannot be fetched, and its claim says so: "<site> reports that …".

## Angles

With four researchers, for example:

1. Definitions and the current state.
2. Data points.
3. Opposing views.
4. Dated events.
